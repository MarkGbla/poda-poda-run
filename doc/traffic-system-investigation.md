# Traffic, speed and collision investigation

23 October 2026. This report records the implementation before the traffic fix.

## Current flow and findings

1. **Player speed and movement.** `src/simulation/systems/PlayerSystem.js` computes a distance-progressive target of 15–33 m/s, multiplied by 1.45 while gas is held and capped by the selected vehicle's `maxSpeed` (25–42 m/s in `src/data/GameConfig.js`). It applies vehicle acceleration, 24–30 m/s² braking for most player vehicles, passive deceleration, stop dwell and CBD corner caps. It advances odometer and stop distance by `speed * dt`; lane input changes `S.lane`, while `S.x` eases toward `LANES[S.lane]` at a `dt` scaled handling rate. `src/main.js` keeps the player at world z=0 and moves scenery/traffic, so this is a **moving world with lateral player movement**, not a player translating forward in z.
2. **NPC speed.** `src/main.js` sets lane cruise speeds to `[10.5, 8, 5.5]` m/s. Some waka/freight variants use 4 m/s. `TrafficAI.js` changes ordinary traffic speed by at most +3 or -10 m/s², including random curb stopping and limited lane changes. `SendMeController.js` separately uses +2.5/-9 m/s² and already treats the player as a leader. At 34 m/s player speed, relative speed is 23.5–30 m/s (85–108 km/h) for common traffic. This explains the slow-moving-wall impression.
3. **Traffic movement.** `TrafficSystem.js` applies `vehicle.z += playerDistance - vehicle.speed * dt`. Negative z is ahead; positive z is behind. `TrafficAI.js` looks for a vehicle with lower z, but excludes the player. Its final separation line is ineffective in normal following and can teleport a follower if it has already crossed its leader. The `src/main.js` reverse obstacle iteration updates AI, movement and collision one object at a time, so a follower can see a mixture of current and previous-frame positions.
4. **Spawning.** `src/main.js:spawnRow` initially places rows from -45 to -189 m, then at -200 m every roughly 18–42 m of player travel. One or two lanes are selected, with a 22 m same-lane spacing rejection; the kerb lane is protected near stops and CBD corners can reject spawns. Spawn distance does not depend on player speed or visible fog distance. The low quality profile's 110 m fog horizon leaves only about 3.7 seconds to a 4 m/s hazard when the player travels at 34 m/s.
5. **Collision and game over.** `CollisionSystem.js` checks a 2D axis-aligned rectangle around the fixed player origin after traffic moves. Jumpable hazards can be cleared above y=.55; crossing pedestrians are ignored below 2 m/s. `src/main.js:hit` removes a struck obstacle, awards collision damage, reduces speed and passengers, and calls `endRun` only when condition becomes fatal. Potholes use `RoadContactSystem.js` instead. A near miss has no separate event/score.
6. **Current collider dimensions.** Player widths/lengths (m): poda 2.1/5.4, kekeh 1.45/2.8, taxi 1.95/4.2, okada .85/1.9, waka 2.5/9. Traffic widths/lengths: kekeh 1.5/2.8, okada .9/1.9, taxi 1.95/4.2, poda 2.2/5.4, waka 2.5/9, freight 2.5/8–12.8, SendMe .85/2.05. Static roadworks are 2.1/8. The collision formula subtracts .3 m from each combined half extent, so effective overlap is already slightly forgiving. Rendered meshes are not the collider source; freight visuals can extend beyond the stated 2.5 m width. The debug view should expose these differences rather than applying an arbitrary global shrink.
7. **Frame time.** `GameClock.js` caps `dt` at .05 s and all active player/traffic movement is `speed * dt`. Attract mode also uses `speed * dt`. A long frame intentionally slows simulated time, but no per-frame speed increment was found.
8. **Tunneling.** Yes. `CollisionSystem.js` checks only final positions. If the relative z displacement crosses the entire collision interval during one frame, a crash can be missed; rapidly changing x during a lane change also makes endpoint-only collision timing uncertain.
9. **Player as leader and stopped-player bug.** SendMe riders see the player. Ordinary vehicles do not. A parked player's z=0 never enters their leader search, so traffic arriving from behind can overlap and `hit` the player. The existing player collision has no direction exception.
10. **Game loop.** `src/main.js:loop` uses `requestAnimationFrame` and `GameClock.tick`. `update` advances player, world, stop logic, spawn, then traffic/obstacle collision. `src/rendering/QualityManager.js` changes rendering quality and fog horizon but not simulation rates.

## Gameplay reference

The [public Danfo Run game](https://danforun.com/) exposes gas and brake, lane steering, hopping and speed in km/h. In a brief live Calabar run, a vehicle and distant road hazards were visible ahead while the bus showed about 75 km/h; seconds later the open road and roadside hazards were still readable at about 57 km/h. This is a visual observation, not a calibrated speed, spacing or collision measurement. The existing `research/danforun` material is prior research. No Danfo Run source behavior or exact traffic values are assumed here.

## Proposed smallest fix

- Give ordinary traffic the same leader concept that SendMe uses, including the player and the nearest vehicle ahead in its lane. Compute a speed-dependent following gap, smoothly brake/recover, and constrain each step so a follower cannot pass through a stopped leader. Retain bounded variation and existing curb stops/lane decisions.
- Bring moving traffic cruise speeds into a range appropriate to the player's 15–42 m/s envelope, while leaving stationary hazards stationary. Keep slower freight and kerb traffic but supply a practical reaction window at the visible horizon. Use a shared lane-oriented update instead of mixed-age per-obstacle updates.
- Add swept player/obstacle collision along the previous-to-current relative position, with lateral interpolation, retaining the existing per-vehicle dimensions and small .3 m forgiveness. Keep pothole, jump, crossing and damage behavior.
- Add a development-only collider and telemetry view behind an explicit query flag. It must not appear for ordinary runs.

## Scope, risks and verification

Modify `TrafficAI.js`, `TrafficSystem.js`, `CollisionSystem.js`, `src/main.js`, and focused simulation tests; add a small debug module only if needed. Do not alter map geometry, controls, scoring, player vehicle upgrades, obstacle types, stops, camera or game-over damage rules.

Risks: higher traffic speeds change perceived difficulty and approach timing; lane changes can produce side contacts; following constraints may produce queues that never restart; fog on low settings limits actual reaction time even when spawn is farther away. Verify deterministic stopped-player and multi-car queue/recovery scenarios, maximum-speed closing time, near misses and swept collisions, and 30/60/120 FPS. Run the existing test/build suite and inspect desktop and mobile layouts where browser tooling permits.

## Implemented and verified

Ordinary vehicles now use the player's position and the nearest overlapping leader, with a speed-dependent headway, bounded acceleration/braking and a per-step minimum gap. The update runs from front to back so followers read current positions. Cruise speeds are now 18–22 m/s by lane; the deliberately slower moving vehicles use 70–78% of those values. At 34 m/s player speed, an ordinary vehicle closes at 12–16 m/s instead of 23.5–28.5 m/s. Stationary road hazards retain zero speed. A visible 110 m low-quality horizon gives about 3.6 seconds to a 12.6 m/s freight vehicle at the absolute 42 m/s player top speed, before collider length is accounted for. This is a calculated bound, not measured human playtest timing.

Player/obstacle collision now sweeps between prior and current relative positions. It retains the original listed collider dimensions and .3 m combined inset. A `?trafficDebug` overlay and wireframes are available only on localhost/127.0.0.1. The overlay shows player/NPC speed, relative speed, nearest gap, lanes, following gap, frame delta and current collision status.

`tests/traffic-behavior.test.cjs` reproduces a parked player with three queued cars, their recovery when the player moves, 30/60/120 FPS consistency, swept high-speed contact and a lateral near miss. All 78 repository tests and `npm run build` passed. `npm run smoke` passed 48 desktop/mobile browser checks with zero page errors. The smoke run ended during its automatic drive, so it did not complete that run's optional pause/resume segment; its other pause/resume and mobile controls checks passed. No manual feel study on a lower-powered physical phone was performed.

### Passing behavior refinement

After player testing, ordinary cars and SendMe motorcycles now seek an open neighboring lane when they approach the player from behind after being passed. They make the decision early enough to move laterally before reaching the player, while the existing following cap remains active until their footprints have separated. If neighboring lanes are occupied, they brake and queue behind the player, then recover when the lane opens or the player moves. The focused tests cover a braking player, safe passing at 30/60/120 FPS, motorcycle passing, and the blocked single-lane queue. The complete suite now has 80 passing tests.
